## The situation

RARO arranges travel for clients across Italy, Greece and the Philippines. Each destination had grown its own way of working: different spreadsheets, different supplier lists, different definitions of what counted as a confirmed booking. Reporting across the business meant someone manually reconciling three versions of the truth every month.

## What we did

We modelled the booking lifecycle once, in a way that held for all three destinations, rather than building three variants. Supplier records, itinerary components and payment milestones became first-class objects, so a booking could be queried and reported on rather than read.

Destination-specific differences were handled through configuration — record types, picklist values and validation — instead of forked logic, which is what keeps the fourth destination cheap to add.

## The result

One process, one definition of a confirmed booking, and reporting that does not require a reconciliation step. The team answers questions in Salesforce that previously required opening three files and comparing them.
